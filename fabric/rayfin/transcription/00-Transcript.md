When you come across a diagram such as this one, you will always see a checkered flag, which signifies the root and serves as the starting point of the topic.

The diagram is referred to as a 'knowledge map'.

The main objective of a knowledge map, first and foremost, is to visualize the topic or concept. The knowledge map is also used to specify how it relates to other sub-topics or sub-concepts. Lastly, the knowledge map will display the directional relationship where they exist.

In addition to a knowledge map, you will see other visualizations throughout this course, and I plan to refer to them in specific terms that are shown on your screen.

A 'Concept' is not related to any one technology and is used to explain the theory behind a concept.

A 'Flow', on the other hand, is related to a specific technology or particular platform, and as the name suggests, it is a diagram to show the directional flow of data between processes or sub-systems.

Whereas a 'Flow' focuses on the movement of data, a 'Sequence' displays the order in which transfer of data and control between components takes place.

Any system is composed of additional sub-systems and sub-components, which are either logical or physical, and their interaction with each other is captured in an 'Architecture' diagram.

Lastly, learners can view the actual UI the end-users interact with in a 'Screenshot' diagram. At times, a screenshot also calls out the sequence in which users interact with the UI. However, screenshot diagrams tend to be static and are not meant to serve as an alternative to viewing the actual UI.
